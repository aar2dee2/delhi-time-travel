How far you go, how far back you get

Straight-line distance from Raisina against the year each site was founded. Time runs left to right, oldest first. The further south you go, the older Delhi gets: about 55 years per kilometre.

The suggested day

    Three double domes, three traditions

    A double dome is two shells, one inside the other. Three buildings on this map have one, and each came from a different tradition. Florence's cathedral dome is older than all three but belongs to a separate line, so it's shown here for comparison only.

    Cutaways are schematic, not to scale: they show how the shells relate, not measured profiles. The construction of Rashtrapati Bhavan's inner dome isn't described in the sources checked. Sources: Archnet (Sikandar Lodi's tomb), University of Washington Silk Road project (Humayun's Tomb), President of India (central dome), Dome of Soltaniyeh.

    The streets are a timeline too

    When New Delhi was laid out in the 1920s, many of its avenues were named after earlier rulers of India, from Ashoka to Aurangzeb, and the rest after viceroys and other Europeans who ruled parts of India. According to the historian Narayani Gupta, Percival Spear later wrote that he had been asked to suggest names from Indian history. Hover a name to find it on the map; where a ruler built something on the map, clicking takes you there.
