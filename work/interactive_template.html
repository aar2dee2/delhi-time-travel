    Three double domes, three traditions

    A double dome is two shells, one inside the other. Three buildings on this map have one, and each came from a different tradition. Florence's cathedral dome is older than all three but belongs to a separate line, so it's shown here for comparison only.

    Cutaways are schematic, not to scale: they show how the shells relate, not measured profiles. The construction of Rashtrapati Bhavan's inner dome isn't described in the sources checked. Sources: Archnet (Sikandar Lodi's tomb), University of Washington Silk Road project (Humayun's Tomb), Aga Khan Trust for Culture (Humayun's Tomb dome), President of India (central dome), Rashtrapati Bhavan (Durbar Hall, “double dome”), Dome of Soltaniyeh.

    The streets are a timeline too

    When New Delhi was laid out in the 1920s and early 1930s, many of its avenues were named after earlier rulers of India, from Ashoka to Aurangzeb, and the rest after viceroys and other Europeans who ruled parts of India. According to the historian Narayani Gupta, Percival Spear later wrote that he had been asked to suggest names from Indian history. Many have been renamed since, some as recently as 2023. The map gives today’s name, with older names in brackets. Hover or tap a road, or a name in the list, to pick it out.
